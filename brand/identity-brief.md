# 7th Creation — Identity Rebuild Brief

> **Founder decision: rebuild properly.** The current logo is treated as a
> direction, not a finished mark.
> Companion to `brand/identity.md` (audit of what exists). This document briefs
> the rebuild. Hand it to a designer, or work from it directly.

---

## 01 — The Strategic Input

The identity has one unusual job, and it drives everything below.

**7th Creation is two things that must read as one.** A publication that must feel
independent and rigorous, and a studio that must feel crafted and desirable.
Prospects have to make the connection unprompted — *"these are the people who wrote
that"* — because that connection is the entire commercial mechanism
(`marketing/growth-plan.md` §4).

An identity that serves only the publication looks austere and undersells the
craft. One that serves only the studio looks like an agency and undermines the
publication's independence. **The mark has to hold both.**

Secondary but real: the studio sells creative work to people who can judge creative
work. The identity is a live sample of the product in a way it simply isn't for most
businesses. It will be assessed by buyers as evidence.

---

## 02 — What Carries Forward

The rebuild is not a blank page. Three things in the current mark are worth keeping.

1. **The 7C monogram.** The numeral 7 whose stroke sweeps into a crescent that
   doubles as a C. Genuinely ownable, resolves both initials into one gesture, and
   recognisable as bare outline — the real test. **Keep the idea; redraw the
   execution.**
2. **The orange A.** The crossbar-less triangle in CREATION. The wordmark's
   fingerprint. Keep, with the constraints in `identity.md` §3.
3. **The aspiration.** Figure, summit, star. It is why the brand feels like a
   creative studio rather than a consultancy, and dropping it entirely would cost
   real warmth. The problem was never the metaphor — it was rendering it
   photographically.

## What Does Not

- The photographic/3D composite treatment. It doesn't scale, and it reads as
  AI-generated to the audience least forgiving of that.
- The metallic gradient as the primary. Flat gold, always; metallic as an occasional
  print finish.
- Two divergent versions with redrawn interiors.
- The unexplained olive green.

---

## 03 — Constraints (non-negotiable)

Any proposed direction must satisfy all of these:

| # | Constraint | Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Legible at 16px | Favicon test — recognisable, not merely present |
| 2 | Works in one colour | Fax test — solid black, no gradients, no tints |
| 3 | One mark, many colourways | No redrawn interiors between versions |
| 4 | Four-tier ladder | Full / Standard / Compact / Icon (`identity.md` §5.2) |
| 5 | Vector-native | Drawn as curves, not traced from a render |
| 6 | Passes contrast | 4.5:1 for any functional use |
| 7 | Reads on both grounds | Light and dark, equally resolved |
| 8 | Holds both engines | Credible on a research report *and* on a studio deck |

Constraint 1 is where the current mark fails hardest, and it should drive the
drawing rather than be checked at the end.

---

## 04 — Three Directions

Presented as genuine alternatives, with a recommendation.

### Direction A — "Refined Ascent" *(recommended)*

Keep the 7C monogram and the scene, but redraw everything as precise geometric line
work. The figure becomes a single confident stroke; the mountains become two or
three clean angular forms; the star becomes a drawn four-point spark. Nothing
photographic, nothing textured.

The interior simplifies progressively down the tiers — full scene at hero size,
figure and star at standard, star alone at compact, bare monogram at icon — so
every tier is the same mark seen at a different distance.

- **Holds both engines:** yes. Line-work rigour reads as editorial; the metaphor
  keeps the warmth.
- **Risk:** the interior must be drawn *very* well. Mediocre line art looks like
  clip art, which is worse than the current render.
- **Why recommended:** keeps the equity and the emotional idea, fixes every
  technical failure, and the discipline of the redraw is itself a demonstration of
  craft — which matters when the identity is read as a work sample.

### Direction B — "Pure Monogram"

Drop the scene entirely. The 7C becomes a precise geometric construction — drawn on
a visible grid, with the crescent's curve mathematically related to the 7's angles.
The star survives only as an optional standalone accent for editorial use.

- **Holds both engines:** partially. Strong for the publication, colder for the
  studio.
- **Risk:** loses the aspiration that makes the brand feel human, and lands closer
  to consultancy than creative studio.
- **Consider if:** the publication becomes clearly dominant, or enterprise
  (`growth-plan.md` §7.5) becomes the primary segment.

### Direction C — "Monogram + Spark"

Middle path. Keep the 7C and the four-point star; drop the figure and mountains.
The star sits in the crescent's negative space as a single bright point.

- **Holds both engines:** yes, leaning editorial.
- **Risk:** the star alone is a much more generic device than the figure-on-summit.
  Weaker differentiation.
- **Consider if:** Direction A's redraw proves too difficult to execute well at the
  Standard tier.

## 05 — Colour

Resolve per `identity.md` §2:

- **Flat gold** replaces metallic as primary. 🔶 Sample the exact value from the
  source; note it appears to match the `#C9A84C` Production X already uses — worth
  making that parent relationship deliberate rather than coincidental.
- **Signal Orange** stays as the single accent. One accent, not two.
- **Drop the olive**, or give it a defined job in both versions. Recommendation:
  drop.
- Add flat gold, all-black, all-white and knockout colourways.
- Fix the gold-on-off-white contrast failure for any functional use.

---

## 06 — Typography

Beyond the wordmark, the identity needs a working type system:

- **Confirm the wordmark face.** 🔶 Geometric sans, appears Futura/Montserrat/Jost family.
- **Editorial face** — for the publication. Should lean toward authority and long-
  form readability. A serif here would do real work: it separates the publication
  from the studio's geometric wordmark while raising its credibility, and the
  contrast between the two is a feature rather than an inconsistency.
- **Interface/body face** — for the site, decks and documents.
- **A rule for the geometric caps** — recommendation: never below 14px, never more
  than five words. Tracked geometric caps become unreadable in quantity.

---

## 07 — Deliverables

1. Master vector artwork, all four tiers × all colourways (SVG)
2. PNG exports at 1x/2x, favicon set (16/32/180/512)
3. Wordmark-alone and monogram-alone lockups
4. Clear-space and minimum-size diagrams
5. Colour specification — hex, RGB, CMYK, Pantone
6. Type specification and licences
7. Misuse examples — the "don't" page, drawn not described
8. All source files committed to `brand/assets/` per its README

---

## 08 — Sequencing

The identity rebuild should follow — not lead — the segmentation decision in
`growth-plan.md` §7.5.

An identity aimed primarily at startups and brands is warmer and more expressive
than one aimed at enterprise. Direction A suits the former; Direction B suits the
latter. Committing to artwork before that decision risks redrawing twice.

**Practical order:** take the segmentation decision → confirm direction from §04 →
commission the redraw → produce the tier ladder → publish usage rules.

---

## 09 — The Test

When a candidate mark exists, apply one question:

> **Does it look like it belongs on a research report and on a studio proposal —
> and would a prospect recognise them as the same people?**

If either half fails, the mark hasn't done the one job that matters.
