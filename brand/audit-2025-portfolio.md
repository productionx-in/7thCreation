# Audit — Photography Portfolio Deck (Feb 2025)

> Source: `7th_creation Photography Portfolio Presentation.pdf`, 11 pages.
> Built in Canva, created 2 February 2025 — roughly 18 months old.
> Supplied by the founder as an "old asset".

---

## 01 — What It Establishes

This is the first hard evidence about the business, and it does not match what the
strategy in this repo was built on.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **7th Creation is** | N.V.R. Sai Kiran, a professional photographer |
| **Based** | India |
| **Services shown** | Landscapes · Product · Baby shoots · Weddings · Industrial |
| **Positioning** | Personal name and company name given equal weight |
| **Clients** | Real and commercial — a consumer brand, a manufacturing plant |

**The work is good.** The product photography is properly lit, high-key commercial
work for a real brand. The industrial shoot — a pharma or chemical plant interior —
is cleanly exposed, well-composed and technically difficult. This is a photographer
who can deliver commercial briefs, not a hobbyist.

That matters, because it means the premium ambition is credible. The raw material
supports it. The presentation does not.

---

## 02 — Problems With the Deck

**1. It is built as a CV, not a portfolio.** Page 2 leads with name, date of birth
and age. A client hiring a photographer does not need his date of birth — that is a
job-application convention, and it frames him as a candidate seeking work rather
than a business selling a service. **Remove it.**

**2. Consumer and commercial work sit side by side.** Baby shoots and wedding
moments appear alongside industrial plant photography and brand product work. These
are different businesses with different buyers and, critically, different price
points — a manufacturing client who sees baby-shoot pricing has been handed an
anchor. This is the exact problem `architecture.md` was written to solve, and it is
live in this deck.

**3. No commercial substance.** No client names, no brief, no outcome, no
testimonial, no process, no indication of what anything costs or how to start. It
shows *that* he can shoot. It does not show what it is like to hire him.

**4. Contact appears only on the final page**, alongside an industrial image, with
no call to action.

**5. Template leakage.** The file's own metadata still carries the Canva template
name — "Black White Minimalist Photography Portfolio Presentation" — which is what
a client's PDF reader displays in the title bar. A small thing that signals
"template" before page one loads.

**6. Brand hierarchy is unresolved.** "7TH CREATION" and "N V R SAI KIRAN" carry
roughly equal weight. Is this a company with a founder, or a person with a trading
name? The deck doesn't decide, and that ambiguity costs on commercial briefs where
a buyer is choosing between a supplier and an individual.

## 03 — What This Means for the Strategy in This Repo

**Plainly: a significant part of what has been built points at the wrong business.**

The strategy assumed a creative studio publishing research into the digital economy
for founders and marketers, positioned against Stratechery and The Information.
That is not this. This is a photographer in India with commercial and consumer
clients, evolving toward "A Creative Studio" — the tagline on the newer logo.

### What does not survive

- **The publication as a "research outlet on the digital economy."** A photographer
  has neither the material nor the standing for that, and his buyers aren't reading it.
- **The media competitive set.** Stratechery, The Information, Lenny's — irrelevant.
- **The full editorial apparatus.** Sourcing standards, correction logs and
  conflicts policies are built for journalism. A photographer publishing his own
  work needs none of it. `editorial/` should be **suspended**, not published.
- **"Operators building in the digital economy"** as the audience.

### What survives — and gets *stronger*

- **"We show our work."** For a photographer this stops being a metaphor. The
  content asset is the shoot itself — the brief, the constraint, the lighting call,
  the problem solved on site. This is the most natural content any business could
  have, and he already generates it every time he works.
- **Case studies showing reasoning.** Phase 1 of the growth plan applies almost
  unchanged, and is better suited here than it was to the imagined business.
- **The segmentation logic** — but pointed at the real split: **commercial
  (product, industrial, brand) vs consumer (wedding, baby).** Consumer work anchors
  commercial pricing downward, exactly as argued. Day 7 was the right mechanism
  aimed at the wrong segment.
- **Raise prices via demonstrated expertise and selectivity.** Unchanged.
- **The identity work.** Unaffected — `identity-brief.md` stands.

### The strategic read, if this is still the business

**The commercial work is the stronger asset and it is buried.**

Industrial and product photography is B2B, repeatable, retainer-capable and priced
on capability. Weddings and baby shoots are consumer, one-off, seasonal, referral-
dependent and priced against every other photographer in the city. The deck gives
them equal billing, which is a pricing decision made by accident.

If the ambition is "creative studio" at premium rates, the commercial work is the
route and the consumer work is what needs containing — under a separate offer, on
the Day 7 model, for exactly the reasons already documented.
