# 7th Creation — Brand Architecture

> Implements the segmentation decision (`marketing/growth-plan.md` §7.5).
> Defines how the studio, the publication and the productised offer relate —
> and the rules that keep the structure doing its job.

---

## 01 — The Model: Branded House, One Endorsed Product

```
                        7TH CREATION
                     (the master brand)
                              │
        ┌─────────────────────┼─────────────────────┐
        │                     │                     │
   THE STUDIO           THE PUBLICATION        DAY SEVEN
   (revenue)            (trust engine)      by 7th Creation
                                            (productised)
   Startups ·           Research, sourced      Creators ·
   Brands ·             and dated. Not         personal brands.
   Enterprise           separately branded.    Fixed price,
   Quoted privately.                           published.
```

Two principles hold this together:

1. **The studio and the publication must read as one entity.** The commercial
   mechanism is a prospect thinking *"these are the people who wrote that."* Any
   architecture that obscures the link breaks the model.
2. **The creator offer must read as a different kind of thing.** Not a cheaper
   tier — a product. Products and engagements aren't compared to each other, which
   is what defuses the price anchoring.

---

## 02 — The Publication: Do Not Give It a Separate Brand

A tempting move at this stage is to name the publication and let it run as its own
property. **Don't.**

A separately-branded publication has to build its own recognition from zero, and
every unit of trust it earns accrues to a name that isn't the one selling the work.
The whole reason the media arm justifies its cost is that its credibility transfers
to the studio — a separate brand is precisely the structure that prevents transfer.

**Do this instead:** publish as 7th Creation. A *format* name is fine — a newsletter
or report series can be "The Brief" or similar — but the format name stays
subordinate and 7th Creation stays dominant on every piece. The masthead should
never leave doubt about who wrote it or that they can be hired.

**Test:** a reader who has never seen your site should be able to name the studio
after one piece. If a format name is doing more work than the master brand, it's
too strong.

## 03 — The Productised Offer

### 03.1 — Naming

The name must sound like a product with a defined shape, not a budget tier. Anything
that reads as "7th Creation Lite" reintroduces the exact anchoring the separation
exists to prevent.

| Name | Rationale | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Day Seven** ⭐ | In the creation narrative the seventh day is completion — so it reinforces the parent name *and* implies a bounded, finished unit of work. Sounds like a format | Slightly abstract until explained once |
| First Light | Dawn and star imagery, straight from the logo. Good for launch work | Weak link to "7"; common in startup naming |
| Ascent | Picks up the summit figure in the mark. Suits creators levelling up | Generic; heavily used |
| Waypoint | A defined marker on a journey; bounded, like the offer | Cold for a creator audience |
| Origin | Natural for identity work | Very common in branding; no link to the parent |

**Recommended: Day Seven.**

It is the only option that reinforces "7th Creation" while sounding like a
*thing you buy* rather than a service level. The completion association does useful
work — it frames the offer as finished and bounded, which is exactly what a
fixed-scope product needs to communicate, and it makes "custom scope" feel
off-brand rather than merely disallowed.

**Lockup:** `DAY SEVEN` with `by 7th Creation` set small beneath. Endorsement, not
co-equality. This is the lockup that needs the Compact/Icon tiers from
`identity-brief.md` §04 to be properly drawn.

### 03.2 — Specification

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **For** | Creators and personal brands |
| **Shape** | Fixed scope, fixed price, fixed timeline. No variations |
| **Price** | 🔶 Published openly. Set it from real delivery cost + margin, not by discounting the studio rate |
| **Scope** | 🔶 A defined asset set, specified once and not negotiated per client |
| **Intake** | Self-serve or near it. A form, not a proposal |
| **Delivery** | Templated process. If it needs a bespoke plan, it isn't this offer |

**The discipline that makes it work:** zero proposal overhead. The moment a
Day Seven engagement requires a custom quote, it has become studio work at studio
prices — or it has become unprofitable. There is no third outcome.

### 03.3 — Its Second Job

Day Seven isn't only creator containment. It also gives inbound studio leads a
low-friction entry point: a prospect who isn't ready for a full engagement can buy
a small, real piece of work, and a share of them will graduate. That makes it a
qualification mechanism as well as a revenue line — it filters before it consumes
proposal time.

## 04 — How Each Segment Is Presented

| Segment | Brand | Pricing | Public case studies | Publication speaks to them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Startups** — brand & identity | 7th Creation | Quoted privately | Yes — lead with these | Directly |
| **Brands** — content & campaigns | 7th Creation | Quoted privately | Yes | Directly |
| **Enterprise** | 7th Creation | Quoted privately | Yes, once they exist | Indirectly — rigour is the signal |
| **Creators** | Day Seven | **Published** | No | Not the primary reader |

The asymmetry in the pricing column is the whole mechanism. **Published price on the
product, private quotes on the engagements.** A buyer can't anchor against a number
they can't see, and the number they *can* see is attached to something visibly
different in kind.

## 05 — The Rules

These are what get tested in a slow quarter. They are worth holding.

**Never**
- Quote creator custom work off the studio rate card. It goes through Day Seven or
  it is declined.
- Put creator work in the studio's public case studies.
- Publish studio pricing. Enterprise quotes are conversations, not list prices.
- Let Day Seven scope drift. Scope creep here doesn't just erode margin — it
  collapses the distinction the architecture exists to maintain.
- Give the publication a brand strong enough to compete with the master brand.

**Always**
- Endorse Day Seven visibly. The halo is the point; hiding the link would forfeit
  the credibility that makes the product sell.
- Route Day Seven graduates to the studio deliberately. That path is a designed
  feature, not an accident.
- Keep the publication unmistakably 7th Creation's.

---

## 06 — When This Gets Tested

Two predictable pressures, and the answer to both:

**"This creator has a big budget, can we just quote them?"** — Then they aren't a
creator client, they're a brand client, and they buy studio work at studio prices
under the 7th Creation name. The segment is defined by what is bought and how, not
by the person's job title.

**"Day Seven is outselling the studio."** — Good, and also a warning. Check whether
the studio pipeline is genuinely weak or whether Day Seven is absorbing leads that
should have been engagements. If it's the latter, the entry point is priced too
close to the studio's floor, or the studio's offer isn't visible enough on its own.

---

## 07 — Review Trigger

Revisit this architecture when any of these happen:

- Enterprise moves from graduate-into to actively won — the balance shifts and the
  creator offer may need more distance
- Day Seven exceeds roughly a third of revenue — it has stopped being a containment
  device and become a business, which needs its own decision
- The publication starts producing leads for Day Seven rather than the studio —
  the register has drifted toward the wrong reader
