## 01 — Core Message

> **7th Creation helps operators in the digital economy make decisions they can
> defend, by publishing research that is sourced, dated and revisited.**

One idea, stated once. Everything below is this sentence at a different altitude.

---

## 02 — Value Proposition

7th Creation is a research publication for people building in the digital economy.
Where most coverage trades in speed and opinion, we publish only what we can
verify — every claim sourced, every piece dated, and every conclusion revisited
when the facts move. The result is material you can act on and still defend three
months later, when someone asks where the number came from.

## 03 — Tagline Options

| Tagline | Style | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Verified, not viral.** | Functional / witty | States the position and the competitive contrast in three words, and commits the brand to a standard readers can hold it to. |
| The digital world, verified. | Functional | Closest to the existing README language; explains the category to a cold reader with no work required. |
| Research for people who ship. | Functional | Owns the audience rather than the method — clearest to a practitioner, weakest against competitors. |
| Signal, sourced. | Witty | Compact and memorable, but "signal vs. noise" is worn thin in this category. |
| Built on what's true. | Emotional / aspirational | Warmest option, and the most future-proof — but vague enough that a hype outlet could also claim it. |

**Recommended: "Verified, not viral."**

It does three jobs at once: names the method, draws the contrast with the entire
competitive set, and — most importantly — makes a promise the brand can be *held
to publicly*. A credibility position needs a claim that would be embarrassing to
break. Taglines that can't be violated also can't be believed.

**The one risk, stated plainly:** defining yourself against a competitor set ties
your identity to theirs, and "viral" may date. If that becomes a concern in
year two, **"The digital world, verified."** is the safe successor — same position,
no dependency on what anyone else does.

## 04 — Messaging Hierarchy

**Level 1 — Brand Headline**

> Research you can defend.

**Level 2 — Supporting Statement**

> 7th Creation covers the digital economy for the people building in it. We publish
> only what we can verify — sourced, dated, and corrected in public when we get it
> wrong.

**Level 3 — Key Messages (the four proof pillars)**

**1. Every claim is sourced and dated.**
The reader can trace any number back to where it came from and see when it was
true. Nothing runs on "reportedly" or an unattributed screenshot.
- *Proof:* Public sourcing standard. Inline citation on every figure. Visible
  publication and last-verified dates on every piece.

**2. Written by people who have shipped.**
Contributors are practitioners, not commentators. The analysis comes from people
who have carried the consequences of the decisions they're writing about.
- *Proof:* Operator bios on every byline, stating what they built and where.

**3. We go back and fix it.**
Most publishing treats the publish button as the end of the obligation. Pieces are
living: when the facts change, the piece changes, and the change is logged.
- *Proof:* Public correction log. Changelog on evergreen pieces. Revisit dates.

**4. We name our conflicts before you ask.**
Sponsors are disclosed, unprompted, on the page. No sponsor has ever seen a piece
before publication, and no sponsorship has ever bought editorial coverage.
- *Proof:* Published conflicts and sponsorship policy. Sponsor disclosure inline,
  not in a footer.

> **Note on pillars 3 and 4:** these are the two nobody else in the reference set
> does consistently, and both are cheap to implement and hard to copy credibly
> after the fact. They should be built first — see `marketing/growth-plan.md`,
> Days 1–30. They are the literal mechanism by which the rate card rises.

## 05 — Messaging by Audience

**To the audience (readers — the asset)**

- **Lead with:** the time it saves and the exposure it removes.
- **Say:** "You can act on this and still defend it in three months."
- **Don't say:** "insights", "must-read", "curated". Readers in this category are
  saturated with those words and discount them automatically.

**To sponsors (the paying customer — the revenue)**

- **Lead with:** who the audience is and how that is verified — never how many.
- **Say:** "You aren't buying impressions. You're buying a named, verified audience
  of decision-makers who can't be reached at this density anywhere else — next to
  editorial they already trust."
- **Don't say:** subscriber counts as the headline number. Leading with volume
  invites CPM comparison, and CPM comparison is the exact conversation that caps
  the price.

**To contributors and hires**

- **Lead with:** the standard. Talented practitioners are drawn to the constraint,
  not the reach.
- **Say:** "We publish less than everyone else and check more than anyone else."

**To partners and platforms**

- **Lead with:** independence. The conflicts policy is a feature in this conversation.
- **Say:** "We'll take your money and we still won't show you the draft."

## 06 — Elevator Pitches

**5 seconds**
> Research-grade coverage of the digital economy. Verified, not viral.

**15 seconds**
> 7th Creation is a research publication for people building digital products.
> Everything we publish is sourced and dated, and we go back and correct it when
> the facts change — so you can act on it and still defend it later.

**60 seconds**
> Most coverage of the digital world is priced and produced on speed. That works
> for awareness and fails completely for decisions — you read four takes, none of
> them sourced, and still have to go check yourself.
>
> 7th Creation is built the other way round. We publish less, source every claim,
> date every piece, and revisit them when reality moves. Our contributors are
> operators who've shipped the things they write about, and we disclose every
> sponsor on the page.
>
> That makes us useless for people who want to be entertained, and close to
> indispensable for people who have to make a call and defend it. It's why our
> readers are the ones making buying decisions — and why sponsors pay a premium to
> sit next to work that's trusted.
