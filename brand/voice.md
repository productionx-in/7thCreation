# 7th Creation — Brand Voice Guide

> Reads from `.agents/brand-context.md`, `brand/positioning.md`, `brand/messaging.md`.
> Written to be handed directly to a writer, editor or contributor.

---

## 01 — Voice Overview

7th Creation writes the way a senior operator briefs a peer they respect: directly,
without preamble, and with the working shown. It assumes the reader is competent
and busy, so it never explains the obvious and never pads to fill a slot. It is
comfortable being boring when the truth is boring, and it treats uncertainty as
information rather than as a gap to paper over — the sentence "we don't know yet,
and here's what would tell us" is a feature of this voice, not a failure of it.
It has authority without swagger: the confidence comes from the citation, not the
adjective.

**Voice Essence**
> 7th Creation sounds like a senior operator briefing a peer — precise, unhurried,
> and unafraid to say what it doesn't know.

---

## 02 — Tone Dimensions

**Formality** — Casual ←——————●———→ Formal
Formal-leaning, but never stiff. Contractions are fine; slang is not. Write as
you'd write to a respected colleague, not to a conference audience.
> ✅ "The number doesn't hold up. Here's why."
> ❌ "It is our assessment that the figure may warrant further scrutiny."

**Energy** — Calm ←●——————————→ Energetic
Firmly calm. This brand never raises its voice. Urgency is conveyed by the facts,
never by the punctuation.
> ✅ "Three of the five vendors we tested failed at this load."
> ❌ "You won't BELIEVE how badly these tools break under load 🤯"

**Humour** — Serious ←————●———————→ Witty
Serious, with dry understatement permitted. Humour arrives as precision, never as
a joke. If a line would work in a stand-up set, cut it.
> ✅ "The migration took four months. The vendor's page says 'in minutes'."
> ❌ "Spoiler: it did NOT take minutes 😂"

**Expertise** — Accessible ←———————●——→ Authoritative
Authoritative but not gatekeeping. Use the correct technical term, then define it
once, in-line, without condescension. Never simplify to the point of inaccuracy.
> ✅ "Retention here means logo retention — accounts kept, not revenue kept."
> ❌ "Retention (basically, how many people stick around!)"

**Warmth** — Distant ←——————●———→ Intimate
Professionally warm. The reader is a colleague, not a friend and not a lead. No
first-name familiarity, no manufactured intimacy, no "hey friend".

## 03 — Voice Qualities

**1. Precise**
Every number has a source and a date. Every qualifier is load-bearing. Vague
language in this brand doesn't read as caution — it reads as bluffing.
- **Do:** "Sign-ups rose 34% between March and June 2026 (company figures, unaudited)."
- **Don't:** "Sign-ups have grown dramatically in recent months."

**2. Candid**
Say the uncomfortable thing, including about ourselves. Candour is the single
behaviour that earns the premium; the moment it's traded away the position is gone.
- **Do:** "We reported this wrong in April. Here's the corrected figure and how it happened."
- **Don't:** Quietly editing the piece and moving on.

**3. Unhurried**
Never sound like we're racing anyone. No "breaking", no "just in", no artificial
deadline pressure. Being late and right is the entire business model.
- **Do:** "Two weeks on, the picture is clearer than it was at launch."
- **Don't:** "BREAKING: everything you need to know about today's announcement."

**4. Useful**
Every piece answers "so what do I do differently?" If it doesn't, it isn't finished.
- **Do:** "If you're under 50k MAU, this doesn't apply to you yet. Here's when it will."
- **Don't:** "It will be fascinating to watch how this space develops."

**5. Self-limiting**
Know where the evidence stops and say so out loud. Stating the boundary of what we
know is what makes everything inside the boundary believable.
- **Do:** "We couldn't verify the enterprise numbers. Treat that section as vendor-supplied."
- **Don't:** Extending a confident tone across evidence that doesn't support it.

**6. Unbranded**
The voice is institutional. No writer performs a personality on the page, and the
publication never becomes a character. Bylines credit expertise; they don't build
fandoms.
- **Do:** "7th Creation tested nine tools over six weeks."
- **Don't:** "So I got my hands on these tools and honestly? I was shook."

## 04 — Vocabulary

**Words we use**
verified · sourced · dated · tested · measured · we found · we couldn't confirm ·
holds up · at scale · in production · trade-off · the evidence suggests ·
as of [date] · we were wrong · here's what would change this · load-bearing ·
material · defensible

**Words we avoid**
game-changing · revolutionary · disrupt · unlock · 10x · insane · wild ·
must-read · deep dive · hot take · thought leadership · synergy · seamless ·
effortless · secret · hack · the truth about · nobody is talking about ·
leverage (as a verb) · curated · insights · reportedly · sources say (without
naming the sourcing standard applied)

**Brand-specific language**
- Readers are **readers**. Not users, subscribers, community, fam or tribe.
- Sponsors are **sponsors**. Not partners — "partner" implies editorial proximity
  we explicitly don't allow.
- Articles are **pieces** or **reports**. Not content. Never content.
- Corrections are **corrections**. Not updates, not clarifications, unless that is
  precisely what they are.
- The publication refers to itself as **7th Creation** or **we** — never "7C",
  never third-person-brand-speak.

## 05 — Writing Style Rules

**Sentence length.** Mostly short to medium. Vary deliberately: a long sentence to
carry a nuanced argument, then a short one to land it. Never two long sentences
back to back.

**Openings.** No throat-clearing. The first sentence carries information. Delete
any opening that begins "In today's fast-moving landscape" or restates the
headline.

**Structure.** Conclusion first, then evidence, then caveats. The reader should be
able to stop after paragraph one and have the answer. This is a briefing format,
not a narrative one.

**Numbers.** Always with a source and an as-of date. A number without provenance
doesn't go in — no exceptions, including in marketing copy about ourselves.

**Attribution.** Attribute or don't assert. "Reportedly" and "sources say" are
banned unless the sourcing standard is named and linked.

**Hedging.** Hedge with specificity, not with mush. "We couldn't verify X" is
strong. "It may perhaps be the case that X" is weak. Say exactly what is unknown
and what would resolve it.

**Emoji.** None in editorial. None in sponsor-facing material. Permitted sparingly
in operational and social contexts, never to carry tone.

**Punctuation.** No exclamation marks in editorial. Em dashes for asides, sparingly.
Oxford comma on.

**Headlines.** Descriptive, not curiosity-gapped. The headline should let a reader
skip the piece if it isn't for them — a curiosity gap trades their trust for one
click, at a brand whose entire asset is their trust.
> ✅ "Nine deployment tools, tested for six weeks: three failed at scale"
> ❌ "We tested nine deployment tools. The results will surprise you."

## 06 — Voice in Context

| Context | Shift |
| --- | --- |
| **Editorial** | Full voice. Maximum rigour. No exceptions to any rule above. |
| **Newsletter intro** | Slightly warmer, still no hype. A colleague's note, not a host's monologue. |
| **Social (LinkedIn/X)** | Same substance, shorter. Lead with the finding, link the source. Never adopt platform-native hype cadence — the contrast *is* the brand. |
| **Sponsor / rate-card material** | Confident and commercial, still precise. Numbers sourced here too. Sell the audience's quality, never its size. |
| **Corrections** | Plainest voice in the brand. State what was wrong, what's right, how it happened. No defensiveness, no apology theatre. |
| **Recruiting contributors** | Lead with the standard and the constraint. "We publish less and check more." |

---

## 07 — The One-Line Test

Before anything ships, ask:

> **Would this sentence survive a reader asking "how do you know that?"**

If not, it isn't ready. That question is the whole voice, compressed.
