# Editorial Standards

**These are public documents.** They are written to be published on the site, in
7th Creation's voice, and read by people deciding whether to trust the work.

They are also the brand's three load-bearing proof points
(`../brand/messaging.md` §07). A claim about rigour that a reader cannot check is
just an adjective — these are what make it checkable.

| Document | What it commits to |
| --- | --- |
| [`sourcing-standard.md`](./sourcing-standard.md) | What we require before publishing; how claims are sourced; what we do when we can't verify |
| [`corrections-policy.md`](./corrections-policy.md) | How we correct in public, and the correction log |
| [`conflicts-policy.md`](./conflicts-policy.md) | The studio/publication conflict, named openly, and how it's managed |

## ⚠️ Timing — do not publish these yet

**The publication does not exist yet.** Standards describing an editorial practice
that has published nothing read as aspiration, not commitment — and the correction
log would be an empty page.

**Publish them with the first research piece**, not before. Until then they are
working documents that define the standard you're building toward.

**Two parts apply immediately**, to the case studies in `marketing/growth-plan.md`
Phase 1:
- The sourcing standard's requirements on claims, numbers and company-supplied
  figures. A case study citing an unsourced result is the same failure as an
  article doing it.
- The conflicts policy's rule that case studies are labelled as case studies, never
  presented as neutral assessment.

## Before publishing these

🔶 markers indicate what must be filled or confirmed:

- Dates on all three
- A contact route for reporting errors and disclosure failures
- A live URL for the correction log — **the log must exist before the policy
  claiming it does goes live**
- Confirm the financial-interests section reflects reality
- Confirm the sponsorship terms match what you'll actually accept

**Do not publish any of these until every commitment in them is one you will keep
on a bad week.** A published standard you breach is worse than no standard —
it converts a neutral position into a documented broken promise, and this brand has
nothing else to trade on.

## Why the conflicts policy leads with the conflict

The two-engine model creates a permanent, structural conflict: a studio publishing
research about the field it sells into. Every reader sophisticated enough to matter
will spot it within a minute.

Naming it first, in the brand's own words, is the only version of that conversation
7th Creation wins. Waiting to be asked is the version it loses.
