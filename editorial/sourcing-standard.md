# Sourcing Standard

*How 7th Creation decides what it is willing to publish.*

**Last updated:** 🔶 [date] · **Applies to:** everything we publish, including our
own marketing.

---

We publish less than most people covering this field. This document explains why,
and what we require of ourselves before something goes out.

It is public because a standard nobody can check isn't a standard. If we fall short
of what's written here, you have grounds to say so — and we'd rather you did.

## What we require

**Every factual claim is attributable.** If we can't say where something came from,
it doesn't run. Not as a hedge, not as "reportedly" — it simply doesn't run.

**Every number carries a source and a date.** Figures go stale, and a number without
an as-of date is close to useless for making a decision. You will always see both.

**Primary over secondary.** We go to the original document, dataset or person rather
than to someone else's account of it. Where we're relying on someone else's
reporting, we say so and link it.

**We name the method.** When we test, measure or compare something, we describe how
— sample, conditions, what we controlled for, what we couldn't.

## Anonymous sources

We use them, but rarely, and never for a claim that carries the piece on its own.

When we do, we tell you what we can: why the source requested anonymity, what
position they're in to know what they're telling us, and whether we corroborated it
elsewhere. An anonymous claim we couldn't corroborate is labelled as exactly that.

We do not grant anonymity to let someone attack a competitor without consequence.

## Company-supplied figures

Vendors, platforms and companies supply numbers about themselves constantly. We use
them, and we always label them.

When a figure is company-supplied and unaudited, the piece says so in the sentence
that contains the figure — not in a footnote. Where we've been able to verify
independently, we say that too.

## When we can't verify

We say so, plainly, in the piece.

"We couldn't confirm this" is not a failure of the work — it is part of the work.
The boundary of what we know is what makes everything inside the boundary worth
reading. A piece that sounds equally confident throughout is telling you nothing
about which parts to trust.

Where a claim is unverified, you'll also see what would settle it, so you can judge
whether to wait.

## Artificial intelligence

We use AI tools in research, the way we use search engines and spreadsheets.

We do not publish AI-generated text as our own analysis, and we do not treat a model's
output as a source. Anything a model surfaces is checked against the primary material
before it informs a word of the piece. Where AI has done something material to a
piece — analysing a dataset, processing a corpus — we describe what it did.

## Dates and revisiting

Every piece carries its publication date and, where relevant, a last-verified date.
Evergreen pieces carry a changelog.

We revisit work when the facts move. See our
[corrections policy](./corrections-policy.md).

## What we won't publish

- Claims we can't source
- Coverage of an announcement that adds nothing but speed
- Anything a sponsor or client has been shown before publication — see our
  [conflicts policy](./conflicts-policy.md)
- A piece written to a schedule when there was nothing verified to say

That last one costs us. A missed week is cheaper than an unsourced piece, so we take
the missed week.

## Holding us to it

If something we've published doesn't meet this standard, tell us: 🔶 [contact].
We'll correct it in public, and the correction will say what went wrong.
