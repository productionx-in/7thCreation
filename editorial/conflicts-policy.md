# Conflicts Policy

*Where our interests could compromise our judgement, and what we do about it.*

**Last updated:** 🔶 [date]

---

## Start with the obvious one

**7th Creation is a creative studio that publishes research about the field it sells
services in.**

That is a conflict. Not a potential one — a structural, permanent one. We write about
disciplines, tools and companies that we also work with, compete against, or would
like to work with.

We're not going to claim otherwise, and we'd distrust anyone in our position who did.
What we can do is be specific about how it's managed, and let you weigh it.

## How we manage it

**We disclose client relationships in the piece.** If we write about a company we've
worked for, currently work for, or have pitched to in the last twelve months, that
appears in the piece itself — in the relevant passage, not in a footer.

**Clients get no editorial influence.** No client sees a piece before publication.
No client can request coverage, prevent coverage, or have a published piece changed
except through the same [corrections process](./corrections-policy.md) as anyone else.

**We don't review our own work.** We don't publish assessments of projects we
delivered, and we don't compare our work favourably to competitors' in editorial. If
we want to show what we made, that's a case study, and it is labelled as one.

**Where the conflict is too sharp, we don't publish.** Occasionally the honest answer
is that we can't cover something usefully. Then we don't, and where it's not
confidential we say why.

## Sponsorship

We largely don't sell it, and that is a deliberate commercial decision rather than a
virtue.

Where we do accept sponsorship — currently only on a flagship annual report 🔶 —
the terms are:

- Disclosed prominently, on the piece, not in a footer
- The sponsor sees nothing before publication
- The sponsor has no say in subject, findings or framing
- We don't accept sponsorship from a company that is the subject of the work

If those terms cost us a sponsor, that's the correct outcome. The reason our analysis
is worth reading is the same reason we can't sell influence over it.

## Gifts, access and review units

We pay for the tools we write about wherever it's possible to do so.

Where a company provides free access, a review unit, or paid travel, we say so in the
piece. We don't return review units in exchange for favourable coverage, and we don't
accept anything conditional on how we cover it.

We don't accept gifts beyond nominal value.

## Financial interests

🔶 *Confirm and complete before publishing.*

Where anyone involved in a piece holds a financial interest in a company it covers,
that is disclosed in the piece. Where the interest is significant enough to make
disclosure insufficient, that person doesn't work on the piece.

## Day 7

Day 7 is our productised offer for creators and personal brands. It is 7th Creation's
own product, and it is disclosed as such wherever it appears in editorial.

We don't publish comparisons of services in a category Day 7 competes in without
saying plainly that we compete in it.

## Contributors

Contributors are practitioners, which means they have clients and employers too. They
disclose the same relationships we do, on the same terms, and we publish those
disclosures with their bylines.

## The test we apply

> **If a reader found this out later, would they feel misled?**

If there's a plausible yes, it gets disclosed. When we're unsure, we disclose — the
cost of an unnecessary disclosure is a slightly longer sentence, and the cost of a
missing one is the only thing this publication actually has.
