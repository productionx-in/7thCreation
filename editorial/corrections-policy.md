# Corrections Policy

*What we do when we get something wrong.*

**Last updated:** 🔶 [date]

---

We will get things wrong. Everyone publishing does. The difference is what happens
next.

Most publications correct quietly — an edit slipped in, no note, no record. We
think that's the wrong instinct. A correction that nobody sees teaches nobody
anything, and it means the archive slowly diverges from what readers remember
reading.

So we correct in public, and we keep the receipts.

## The three levels

**Clarification.** The facts were right, the wording made them easy to misread. We
rewrite for clarity and note the change at the foot of the piece.

**Correction.** Something was factually wrong. We fix it in place, mark the corrected
passage, and add a dated note saying what it previously said, what it says now, and
how the error happened. The original claim stays visible in the note — a correction
that hides what was wrong isn't a correction.

**Retraction.** The piece's central claim doesn't hold. We don't delete it. We mark
it retracted at the top, leave the body intact and struck through, and publish an
explanation. Deleting would be tidier and would also be a way of pretending it never
ran.

Typos and broken links we just fix.

## Every correction is logged

Corrections appear in a single public log 🔶 [link], newest first, with the date, the
piece, and what changed. You can read the whole history of our mistakes in one place.

That is uncomfortable by design. A visible correction log is the cheapest honest
signal a publication can offer, and the fact that most don't keep one is itself
informative.

## Living pieces

Some work is meant to stay useful for years. Those pieces carry a changelog and a
last-verified date, and we go back to them on a schedule rather than waiting for
someone to notice they've aged.

A changelog entry is not an admission of error. It usually means the world moved.
Both are worth recording, and we distinguish between them so you can tell which
happened.

## Timing

We correct as soon as we've confirmed the error — not at the end of the week, not
bundled with other changes.

Where an error was material and the piece was widely read, the correction goes out
through the same channels the piece did. Correcting somewhere quieter than you
published is not correcting.

## Telling us

🔶 [contact]. You don't need to be polite about it, and you don't need to be certain.

If you're right, we'll fix it and say so. If you're wrong, we'll tell you why, and
that exchange sometimes turns into a better piece than the original.

## What we won't do

- Silently edit a factual claim
- Delete a piece to avoid correcting it
- Describe a correction as an "update" when it was an error
- Correct in a place quieter than where we published
- Argue about a mistake in public rather than fixing it
