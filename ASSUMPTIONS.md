# Assumptions Register

Everything in `brand/` and `marketing/` is built on six inputs. Four were confirmed
by the founder. Two were chosen by me because the work is useless without them —
they are strategically coherent with the confirmed answers, but they are **not
researched facts**.

This file exists so the inferred parts stay visible instead of hardening into
assumed truth.

## ⚠️ Contradiction — unresolved

**The logo tagline reads "A CREATIVE STUDIO". Assumption 1 says "media / content brand".**

The founder selected "Media / content brand" when asked, with "Digital studio /
agency" available as the first option — then supplied a logo whose own tagline says
studio. The README's "digital world" wording doesn't settle it either way.

These are not variations on one business. They have opposite revenue logic:

| | Media brand | Creative studio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What's sold | Audience attention | Client work |
| Who pays | Sponsors, advertisers | Clients |
| Audience is | The product | The marketing |
| "Raise prices" means | Higher sponsor rates | Higher day rates / project fees |
| Growth means | More qualified readers | More qualified leads |

**What survives either answer** (roughly 70% of the current work): the strategic
spine — monetise trust rather than reach; raise revenue per unit rather than unit
count; make rigour verifiable so it can be priced. The voice guide, most of the
positioning territory, and the whole of `brand/identity.md` hold up unchanged.

**What breaks if it's a studio:** `marketing/growth-plan.md` §7 (the revenue
ladder is built on sponsorship), §3 Acquisition (a studio acquires *leads*, and a
media property becomes a channel rather than the business), and the ARPR north star
in §10 — which would become revenue per client or per engagement.

**Third possibility, and the most likely one:** a creative studio building a media
property as its top-of-funnel. That is a common and strong model — the publication
earns the trust, the studio monetises it at a much higher rate than sponsorship
ever could. If that's the shape, almost everything here stands, but it gets
re-pointed: the audience work feeds a client pipeline instead of a rate card.

**Status: blocking.** Flagged to the founder.

## Not yet known — needed before execution

These aren't assumptions, they're gaps. The growth plan flags each with 🔶.

- Current audience size, and how much of it is owned (email) vs. rented (platform)
- Revenue by line, current rate card, sponsor renewal rate
- Whether the website exists, and where the audience currently lives
- Team size and publishing capacity
- Whether refusing paid placement and hype coverage is commercially acceptable
  (`marketing/growth-plan.md` §11.4 — this one is load-bearing, not a detail)

## A note on method

The strategy is deliberately built so that the *inferred* inputs are correctable
without discarding the *confirmed* ones. The spine — monetise trust rather than
reach, raise revenue per reader rather than reader count, make rigour verifiable
so it can be priced — follows from the four confirmed answers alone. Changing
assumption 5 changes who the work speaks to and where it's distributed. It does
not change the commercial logic.
